The problem with cloud agents

The mobile-agent pattern is now standard — but every mainstream option routes your source through a vendor's cloud to do it.

📤

Source leaves the building

Repository contents are uploaded to the vendor to give the agent context. For finance, defence, health, legal and gov, that breaks data-residency and IP controls.

🔒

Single-vendor lock-in

The mobile app only drives that vendor's agent. You can't standardise one control plane across Claude, Codex, Gemini and in-house models.

📃

No clean audit story

Who ran which agent against which repo, from which device, when? With a third-party cloud in the path, the answer lives in someone else's logs.

How Mastward keeps it in-boundary

The agent executes on your own host (developer workstation, on-prem box, or your private cloud). Mastward is the control plane — it never adds a cloud hop of ours.

🏛️

Runs on your infra

Self-host the engine on hardware or a tenancy you own. The repo and the agent stay inside your boundary; Mastward has no relay server in the path.

🤖

Vendor-neutral

One cockpit for Claude Code, Codex, Gemini, Aider and local Ollama models. Standardise the workflow without standardising the vendor.

🔑

Per-device access + revoke

Every device is a named, revocable key. Lose a laptop, revoke it instantly. No shared accounts, no vendor identity provider required.

📊

Audit log

Who, what agent, which host, which device, when — recorded in your own audit trail, on your own storage.

🛡️

Secret-masking

Real-time masking of known secrets in terminal output (best-effort defense-in-depth, not a guarantee).

📵

Air-gap friendly

Works on your own network, or a fully offline network. No outbound dependency to use it.
